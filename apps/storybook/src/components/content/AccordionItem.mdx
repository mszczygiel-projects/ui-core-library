# AccordionItem

<ComponentOverview name="AccordionItem" />

## Usage

Use AccordionItem only as a section of an Accordion, which gives it the keys between headings and closes the others when it opens.

## Anatomy

The **base** holds the **heading**, an HTML heading with the **trigger**, a button with the **label** and the **icon**, and the **panel**, a region that holds the content. The web component exposes these parts; React uses `.ui-accordion-item__<part>` classes. Figma names the layers the same.

## Variants & tone

AccordionItem has one appearance. The icon is a chevron that turns over when the section opens.

## Sizes & density

AccordionItem has one size, which follows the surrounding density through its tokens.

## States

<FrameworkCanvas react={ReactStories.Open} webComponents={WebComponentStories.Open} />

`open` shows the content. `disabled` keeps the section as it is and removes its heading from keyboard navigation.

<FrameworkCanvas react={ReactStories.Disabled} webComponents={WebComponentStories.Disabled} />

## Content & labels

`label` is the text of the heading. The content goes into the section as children or into its default slot, and stays in the page while the section is closed.

## Behaviour

A press on the heading opens and closes the section, and so do Enter and Space. The web component dispatches the cancelable `ui-open-change` with `detail.value` and `detail.reason`; its `open` attribute supplies the initial state and follows the section. React offers `defaultOpen`, or `open` with `onOpenChange(open, details)`. Find in page opens a closed section with the reason `find`, whatever the page answers.

## Accessibility

A section of the [accordion](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/accordion/) pattern: a heading of `headingLevel`, from 2 to 6 and an `h3` by default, that holds a button with `aria-expanded` and `aria-controls`, and a region named by the heading. Accordion handles the arrow keys, Home and End.

## Surfaces & themes

The section follows Light and Dark and the default, subtle, inverse and primary surfaces.
