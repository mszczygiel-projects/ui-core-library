# Badge

<ComponentOverview name="Badge" />

## Usage

Use Badge for a short, static mark next to the thing it describes: a status such as “Active” or “Failed”, a category, a count, or a highlight such as “New”. Keep its text to a word or two. Badge is not interactive: use Chip for a choice a user turns on and off or removes, and Notification for a message.

Do not let colour carry the meaning alone. The text, or the `label` of a badge with only an icon, says what the colour means.

## Anatomy

The **base** is the surface of the badge, its fill and corners. It holds an optional **icon** and the **label**, the text. The web component exposes these as parts; React uses the matching `.ui-badge__<part>` classes, and Figma names the layers the same way.

## Variants & tone

<FrameworkCanvas react={ReactStories.Variants} webComponents={WebComponentStories.Variants} />

`tone` says what a badge means: `neutral`, the default, for categories and counts; `brand` for a highlight; and `info`, `success`, `warning` and `error` for a status. `variant` sets the emphasis: `solid`, the default, is a strong fill for marks that must stand out, and `subtle` a light tint for statuses in lists and tables.

## Sizes & density

`medium`, the default, sits next to body text; `small` sits next to small text and inside dense rows. Density changes the height, the padding and the icon without changing the prop. `shape` gives a pill, `rounded` by default, or small corners with `square`.

<FrameworkCanvas react={ReactStories.Shapes} webComponents={WebComponentStories.Shapes} />

## States

Badge has no interactive states. It does not take focus, and its colours change only with the theme and the surface.

## Content & labels

<FrameworkCanvas react={ReactStories.WithIcon} webComponents={WebComponentStories.WithIcon} />

Write the text in the default slot, or as `children`. Supply one trusted SVG in the `icon` slot, or in the `icon` prop; Badge sizes it, colours it with the text and hides it from assistive technology. The icons package provides SVG strings; insert them as markup, such as with `innerHTML` or React's `dangerouslySetInnerHTML`.

A badge with an icon and no text is a square of its height. Give it a `label`, which names it as an image. Badge renders no text of its own, so it has no labels in the configuration.

## Behaviour

- Badge chooses its layout from its content: text, an icon and text, or only an icon. No prop switches it.
- An unknown `tone`, `variant`, `size` or `shape` falls back to the default.

## Accessibility

- **Pattern.** No WAI-ARIA pattern applies: a badge is static text, read in the flow of the page.
- **Name.** The text names a badge. A badge with only an icon has the role `img` and the name of `label`; an author's `aria-label` wins over it. The icon is decorative.
- **Role of the page.** A role such as `status` that the page gives a badge stays, also when it shows only an icon, so that its changes are still announced. The web component writes no role or ARIA attribute on its host.
- **Contrast.** Text has a contrast of at least 4.5 to 1 with its fill for every tone and variant, in Light and Dark, on every surface.
- **Preferences.** In forced colours, an outline keeps the shape of a badge whose fill disappears.

## Surfaces & themes

Colours follow the surrounding theme and surface. The feedback tones take the feedback roles: `solid` the strong colour with the text role on it, `subtle` the light colour with the text role on the light colour. `brand` and `neutral` take the brand and background roles.

## Implementation notes

- The web component watches its slots to choose its layout, and follows an `aria-label` on its host without writing one.
- React chooses the layout from its `icon` and `children`, and passes other attributes of a `span` to its root.
- The Figma set has the design-only property `content` for the three layouts, which code derives from the content.
