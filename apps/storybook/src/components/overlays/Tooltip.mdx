# Tooltip

<ComponentOverview name="Tooltip" />

## Usage

Use Tooltip for a short text that describes a control, such as what an icon button does or the shortcut of a command.

A tooltip shows nothing on touch, so never put information there that the user cannot find another way. Use Popover for content with controls or links, and a hint of a field for text the user needs while filling it.

## Anatomy

The **base** holds the trigger, the child of the tooltip, and the **bubble**, which holds the text and, with `showArrow`, the **arrow**. The web component exposes these as parts; React uses the matching `.ui-tooltip__<part>` classes. Figma names the layers the same way, and its `content` text property is the text.

## Variants & tone

<FrameworkCanvas react={ReactStories.Placements} webComponents={WebComponentStories.Placements} />

`placement` sets the side of the trigger: `top`, the default, `bottom`, and `start` and `end`, which follow the direction of the text. The bubble is centred on its trigger and opens on the opposite side when the window has no room. `showArrow` adds an arrow that points at the middle of the trigger.

## Sizes & density

The bubble is as wide as its text, up to its maximum width, and the text wraps beyond it.

## States

A tooltip is shown or hidden; it has no states of its own.

## Content & labels

`content` is plain text. A tooltip without text shows nothing and describes nothing. The trigger is one element that takes the focus, such as a Button or an IconButton, and keeps its own accessible name: the tooltip describes it, never names it.

## Behaviour

- A mouse resting on the trigger shows the bubble after the delay of `tooltip/show-delay`; the keyboard focus shows it at once. A press that focuses the trigger shows nothing.
- The bubble stays while the pointer moves onto it, and hides after the delay of `tooltip/hide-delay` when the pointer leaves both; it hides at once when the focus leaves and on a press.
- Escape hides it; inside a modal, Escape hides the tooltip first, and the modal stays open.
- One tooltip shows at a time. While one shows, or just after it hides, the next opens at once.

## Accessibility

- **Pattern.** Tooltip follows the [WAI-ARIA tooltip pattern](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/tooltip/): the text has the role `tooltip` and describes its trigger.
- **Keys.** The focus shows it and Escape hides it. The tooltip itself never takes the focus.
- **Names.** The trigger gets the text as its accessible description, through `ariaDescribedByElements` in the web component and `aria-describedby` in React. Button, IconButton and LinkButton pass it to their native control.
- **Contrast.** The text keeps a contrast of at least 4.5 to 1 on the bubble, in Light and Dark. In forced colours the bubble has an outline.
- **Preferences.** The bubble stays while the pointer is over it, and Escape dismisses it, as WCAG 1.4.13 asks.

## Surfaces & themes

The bubble uses the inverse surface: dark in Light, light in Dark, through the Components tokens of tooltip.

## Implementation notes

- Both open the bubble with the native `popover="manual"` attribute in the top layer, and Floating UI places it. A group shared by every copy of the library on the page shows one tooltip at a time.
- The web component keeps its text in its light DOM, in its `content` slot, because an element reference cannot reach into a shadow root. It sets `ariaDescribedByElements` on its trigger; a browser without ARIA element reflection shows the text but gives the trigger no description.
- React clones its child to add `aria-describedby` and a ref, keeping the description and the ref of the page.
- The Figma set has the variant property `showArrow` and the text property `content`; `placement` lives only in code.
