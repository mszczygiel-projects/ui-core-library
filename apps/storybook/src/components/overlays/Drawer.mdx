# Drawer

<ComponentOverview name="Drawer" />

## Usage

Use Drawer for secondary content that the user opens beside the page and closes again: filters of a list, the navigation of a small window, the details of an item. It slides in from an edge over a backdrop, and the page waits until it closes.

Use Dialog for a decision that must stop the user, and keep content that the user needs while working on the page in the page itself.

## Anatomy

The **base** is the panel, a native `<dialog>`, with its backdrop. The **header** holds the **close** button, and the **body** holds the content and scrolls. A bottom drawer that can be dragged shows the **grabber** above the header. The web component exposes these as parts; React uses the matching `.ui-drawer__<part>` classes, and Figma names the layers the same way.

## Variants & tone

<FrameworkCanvas react={ReactStories.Placements} webComponents={WebComponentStories.Placements} />

`placement` sets the edge: `end`, the default, and `start` follow the direction of the text, so `end` is the right edge in English and the left in Arabic; `bottom` rises from the bottom of the window. A side drawer is as tall as the window; a bottom drawer is as tall as its content, up to the window.

## Sizes & density

A side drawer has the width of its token, up to the window; a bottom drawer fills the width. Density changes the padding and the gaps.

## States

A drawer is open or closed. The close button has the states of IconButton.

## Content & labels

Nothing visible names a drawer, so `label` is required; an `aria-label` of the page names it above `label`, and React also accepts `aria-labelledby`. The content goes into the default slot, or `children`, and the actions into the content.

The close button is named by `closeLabel`, then `labels.drawer.close`, “Close” in English. `hideCloseButton` removes it, and its header with it.

<Source language="ts" code={`configureUiCore({ labels: { drawer: { close: 'Zamknij' } } });`} />

## Behaviour

<FrameworkCanvas react={ReactStories.WithForm} webComponents={WebComponentStories.WithForm} />

- The page opens the drawer with `open`. It opens as a modal in the top layer, the rest of the page becomes inert and stops scrolling, and the width of the page stays.
- The focus moves to the element marked with `autofocus` or `data-autofocus`, else to the first focusable element of the content, else to the panel. It returns where it was when the drawer closes.
- Escape, a press on the backdrop, the close button, a form with `method="dialog"` and a drag down of a bottom drawer ask it to close, with a `reason`, as Dialog does. The web component closes itself after `ui-open-change`, which the page may cancel; React reports `onOpenChange(false, details)`.
- `dragToDismiss` lets the user drag a bottom drawer down by its grabber or its header: past a quarter of its height, or with a flick, it closes; otherwise it returns. It has no effect on a side drawer.
- Setting `open` does not report a request. A drawer and a dialog open together share one lock of the scroll.

## Accessibility

- **Pattern.** Drawer follows the [WAI-ARIA dialog (modal) pattern](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/dialog-modal/): a native modal dialog that makes the rest of the page inert.
- **Keys.** Tab and Shift+Tab move through the drawer; Escape asks it to close.
- **Names.** `label` names the drawer. The grabber is decorative: every way to close stays for every user.
- **Contrast.** The content has a contrast of at least 4.5 to 1 on the panel, in Light and Dark.
- **Preferences.** With reduced motion, the drawer opens without sliding or fading.

## Implementation notes

- The web component renders the native dialog in an open shadow root and reflects only `open`; it writes no role or ARIA attribute on its host. `ui-open-change` bubbles, so check `event.target` when a component inside the drawer sends its own.
- React renders the drawer in place, without a portal, closed on the server, and opens it after hydration. The ref is the native dialog; open and close it through `open`. Mark the element to focus with `data-autofocus`.
- The icons come from the icons package, so a consumer's icon set must hold `close`.
- The Figma set has the variant properties `placement` and `hideCloseButton` and the boolean `dragToDismiss`.
